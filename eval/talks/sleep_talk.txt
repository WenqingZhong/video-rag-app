Hello and welcome. Today's talk is about sleep, and why it matters more than most of us think.

Let's start with how sleep is organised. A full period of sleep is not one long state. It is made of cycles, and each cycle lasts about ninety minutes. Most adults go through four to six cycles before waking.

Each cycle has stages. There is light sleep, then deep sleep, and then REM sleep, which stands for rapid eye movement. Most vivid dreaming happens during REM sleep. Deep sleep is when the body releases growth hormone and repairs tissue.

How much sleep do we need? For most adults, the recommendation is seven to nine hours of sleep. Teenagers need more, about eight to ten hours.

Sleep is also important for memory. During deep sleep, the brain replays what we learned during the day and moves it into long-term memory. Students who sleep after studying remember more than students who stay awake.

Now, a few things that make sleep worse. The first is caffeine. Caffeine has a half-life of about five hours. That means a coffee at four in the afternoon is still half active at nine in the evening.

The second is light from screens. Bright light in the evening delays the release of melatonin, the hormone that tells your body it is time to rest.

What about naps? A short nap of about twenty minutes can improve alertness without making you groggy. Longer naps can reach deep sleep, and waking up from deep sleep leaves you feeling worse.

Here is my one piece of advice: keep the same wake-up time every day, even on weekends. A regular schedule is the simplest way to sleep better.

To sum up: sleep in cycles of about ninety minutes, aim for seven to nine hours, watch the caffeine, dim the screens, and keep a regular schedule. Thank you for listening, and sleep well.
